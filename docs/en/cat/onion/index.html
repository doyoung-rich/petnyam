Onion: do not feed; check suspected ingestion with a veterinarian.

Why this recommendation?

This is an Allium food with red-blood-cell injury risk, including cooked or powdered forms.

What to prepare or check

No safe feeding preparation is provided here. Record the product, amount and time.

When not to offer it

Do not wait for signs to declare safety or induce vomiting at home; a veterinarian decides care.

More detail for this food

Do not feed onions to cats. Merck identifies cats as sensitive to Allium foods and describes poisoning from raw, cooked and dried forms.

What if I remove onion pieces from soup?

Removing visible pieces does not establish the absence of onion ingredients. Check sauces, soups and powders.

My cat already ate some

Keep the label and record the estimated amount and time. Contact a veterinarian; this page does not calculate a safe dose or provide home treatment.

Already eaten? Start here

An “avoid” recommendation does not mean every accidental bite is poisoning. Ingredients, amount, time and the animal matter.

  1. Stop further access and keep the food or packaging.
  2. Record the species, product, estimated amount and time; say when an amount is unknown.
  3. For a known hazardous ingredient, or unusual weakness, tremors or breathing, contact a veterinarian promptly. Do not wait for symptoms after a known toxic exposure.

Do not induce vomiting or give human medicines on your own.
