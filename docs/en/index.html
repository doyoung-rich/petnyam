    Start with the evidence

    Food safety depends on the animal and ingredients

    PetNyam summarizes public veterinary and animal-welfare references. It is not a veterinary clinic, emergency service or individualized feeding plan. A food name alone cannot establish safety.

    Can dogs eat grapes? Avoid raisins too

    Grapes and raisins can injure a dog’s kidneys. Do not offer them as treats. If ingestion is suspected, contact a veterinarian even if your dog looks well.

    Can dogs eat chocolate? The product type matters

    Do not feed chocolate to dogs. Theobromine and caffeine are hazards; cocoa powder and dark chocolate differ in concentration from milk chocolate.

    Can dogs eat xylitol? Check sugar-free products

    Xylitol is dangerous to dogs and may cause low blood sugar and liver injury. Contact a veterinarian immediately if ingestion is suspected. Sugar-free does not mean safe for a dog.

    Can cats eat onions? Cooked and powdered forms matter

    Do not feed onions to cats. Merck identifies cats as sensitive to Allium foods and describes poisoning from raw, cooked and dried forms.

    Can cats eat grapes? Avoid offering grapes or raisins

    Cornell includes grapes and raisins among human-food hazards for cats. We follow that precaution without turning dog toxicity data into a feline safe-dose claim.

    Can rabbits eat carrots? An occasional treat, not a staple

    Carrots alone are not an appropriate rabbit diet. RSPCA recommends hay and grass as the dietary foundation, with small occasional portions of root vegetables such as carrots.
