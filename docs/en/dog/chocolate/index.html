Can dogs eat chocolate?

Do not feed

Chocolate: do not feed; check suspected ingestion with a veterinarian.

Plain ingredient guidance; processed products need a separate check.
WhyPreparationAvoidAlready eatenSources

Why this recommendation?

Theobromine and caffeine are hazards; concentrations differ between products, so identify the actual chocolate.

What to prepare or check

No safe feeding preparation is provided here. Record the product, amount and time.

When not to offer it

Do not wait for signs to declare safety or induce vomiting at home; a veterinarian decides care.

More detail for this food

Do not feed chocolate to dogs. Theobromine and caffeine are hazards; cocoa powder and dark chocolate differ in concentration from milk chocolate.

What about chocolate cookies?

A cookie’s total weight does not identify its chocolate content. Check sugar-free products for xylitol as well. Keep the packaging and remaining product.

Can I wait if my dog looks fine?

Signs may not appear immediately. Contact a veterinarian with the weight, product, estimated amount and time. This page does not calculate a toxicity threshold or provide home treatment.

Already eaten? Start here

An “avoid” recommendation does not mean every accidental bite is poisoning. Ingredients, amount, time and the animal matter.

  1. Stop further access and keep the food or packaging.
  2. Record the species, product, estimated amount and time; say when an amount is unknown.
  3. For a known hazardous ingredient, or unusual weakness, tremors or breathing, contact a veterinarian promptly. Do not wait for symptoms after a known toxic exposure.

Do not induce vomiting or give human medicines on your own.
