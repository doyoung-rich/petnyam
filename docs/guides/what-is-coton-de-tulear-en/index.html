What Is a Coton de Tulear? Temperament, Shedding and What to Know Before Getting One

If you found the Korean search term “꽃동강아지,” you may have wondered whether it names a different breed. It is sometimes used when searching for the Coton de Tulear, the breed introduced in this guide.

What is a Coton de Tulear?

The Coton de Tulear has a history associated with Madagascar. “Coton” is French for cotton, while “Tulear” refers to a city in Madagascar. AKC explains the breed's name and history.

Its full white coat gives it a soft, fluffy appearance that makes the name easy to understand. An appealing photograph, however, cannot show all the care involved in sharing everyday life with a dog.

What is the Coton's temperament like?

The Coton is generally described as a cheerful, playful companion that enjoys being close to people. AKC's breed introduction highlights that companionship. It may interest people looking for a dog with whom to build a close family bond.

Not every dog of a breed has the same personality. Individual tendencies and the environment in which a dog grows up matter. Rather than assuming all Cotons will behave alike, ask about the particular dog's behavior and life history.

My own Coton is lively but barks a lot. At dog cafés, my dog plays energetically and people sometimes look with curiosity. I think my dog is quite sweet, but that is my experience with one dog, not a description of every Coton. I shared more in our Coton caregiver's everyday-life account.

Do Cotons shed?

Shedding is a common question among people researching Cotons. The USACTC ownership guide describes relatively little shedding alongside a tendency for the coat to tangle during its transition to an adult coat. It is an older archive; we have not repeated its historical prices or health claims as current facts.

Less shedding does not mean effortless coat care. Long hair can tangle and mat, so regular grooming still needs time and attention. If you are considering a Coton, think about brushing and grooming appointments rather than deciding solely on a low-shedding description.

Tell a groomer about the coat's current condition and what you can realistically manage at home, and ask for a practical demonstration. Do not treat a low-shedding description as a guarantee against human allergies.

What to consider before getting a Coton

Beyond appearance, ask whether your household can provide consistent care. Activity, feeding and healthcare matter alongside the coat.

  • Can you make time to learn and maintain brushing and grooming?
  • Who handles walks and meals when the household is busy?
  • Have you planned grooming, food and veterinary costs using actual consultations and quotes?
  • Have you discussed this dog's health history and current behavior?
